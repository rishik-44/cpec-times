[HEADING: Bashing With Fun: Recap of the Rescheduled Back to School Bash]
by Austin Hall and Carlos Aguilar [BR] [BR]

On Friday, September 18, 2026, we had our rescheduled Back to School Bash during our 45 
minute lunch period. It was filled with fun, laughter, and games from different events such as 
basketball, volleyball, and drawing. [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: week2image3.jpg]
[CAPTION: Sophomore Tyren Rushin nails a three pointer before a basketball game. [BR]Photo Credits: Wyatt Arnold]

Basketball was a popular event during the bash, and there were some fierce games. From one v 
ones to three v threes to even a six v six, basketball at the bash had it all. Students from every 
grade participated, and as you might have expected, the tall upperclassmen dominated over 
the smaller underclassmen. Luckily for those underclassmen, teams were mixed with teens 
from each grade. There were some thrilling moments, from draining contested threes to game 
winning layups, these games were close. It started off as small three v threes, but towards the 
end of the bash, there was a large six v six. Despite its chaos, basketball at the bash was fun. 
[BR] [BR][BR][BR][BR][BR][BR][BR][BR]

[IMAGE: week2image1.jpg]
[CAPTION: Junior Brighton McClamb returns the volleyball over the net during a volleyball game. [BR] Photo Credits: Wyatt Arnold]


Volleyball was by far the most popular event at the bash. The event consisted mainly of 
freshmen and sophomores, with only a couple of juniors, and as with basketball, teams were 
mixed with players from different grade levels. Throughout the whole bash, there were around 
10 players on each side, leading to a quite unorganized game, but fun nonetheless. From 
powerful serves to incredible digs, volleyball at the bash had it all. [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: week2image4.jpg]
[CAPTION: Photo Credits: Wyatt Arnold]

Another thing here at the Back to School Bash was the snack table. They had a variety of 
things, such as sodas, chips, and candy. When the CPEC Times asked senior Emma 
Botzenmayer about the snack table, Emma said that they had a lot of fun and sales. [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: week2image2.jpg]
[CAPTION: Principal Turner hits the volleyball with some students. [BR] Photo Credits: Wyatt Arnold]

It wasn’t just the students that participated in the Back to School Bash. Some of our very own faculty members such as Mr. Freeman 
and Principal Turner participated in events. Principal Turner participated in a three v three, in which he lost due to the poor 
defense of his teammates, along with participating in volleyball. Mr. Freeman participated in multiple one v ones, and those who 
decided to challenge him probably wish that they didn’t. [BR] [BR]

All in all, the bash was a very fun event that brought the school together for a joy-filled lunch period.

[HEADING: Will PSG Win the Champions League for the Third Year in a Row?]
By Cole Botzenmayer [BR][BR]

Paris Saint-Germain have won back to back UEFA Champions League titles. In the 2024-25 season, PSG beat Inter Milan five to nil. In the 2025-26 season PSG won four to three on penalties over Arsenal. Even after having two extraordinary seasons, this year PSG has not had their best start in Ligue 1. [BR] [BR]

On the first match day of Ligue 1, PSG played Rennes with two goals in the first half by Rennes. Luckily, Ferran Torrres made a huge debut for PSG scoring a brace to level out the game. While many thought that PSG would be better next match, PSG tied with Lille at their home stadium scoring their two goals in stoppage time. On matchday three, PSG played Monaco and lost in their home stadium two to one, still not winning a single game that season. Then on September 9, PSG finally won a match scoring 6 goals against Slovan Bratislava with Ferran Torres getting a hat-trick and Ousamane Dembele with a brace. [BR] [BR]

This year in the UCL, PSG played Slovan Bratislava and won six to one with Ferran Torres getting a hat-trick and Ousamane Dembélé with a brace. In the past, we have seen clubs that were ok or even terrible in their domestic league, but when it came to the Champions League, they dominated. A great example of this is Tottenham Hotspur last season. The Spurs, as they are called, finished fourth at the end of league play in the Champions League. However, they finished seventeenth in the English Premier League, just barely escaping relegation. PSG could be on a similar track, but hopefully for the sake and sanity of their supporters they don’t almost get relegated. [BR] [BR]

But back to the original question: Will PSG win the Champions League for the third year in a row? Honestly, probably not. The “threepeat” has only been accomplished four times in UCL history: Real Madrid from 1956 to 1960 (technically three threepeats all at once) and again from 2016 to 2018, Ajax from 1971 to 1973, and Bayern Munich from 1974 to 1976. Those teams had a lot of talent to be able to pull those off. But outside of the 2025 Ballon d’Or winner Ousmane Dembélé, PSG does not have that same talent to pull it off. Sure, striker Ferran Torres is doing great, but last season at FC Barcelona, he put up very pedestrian stats. With all things considered, it is not very likely that PSG will return from Madrid this season with another UCL trophy. [BR] [BR] [BR]

[HEADING: Checking in with the New Teachers]
By Marley Estela Cruz [BR] [BR]

Starting at a new school can be weird. You have to learn where everything is, figure out who everyone is, and somehow remember a million names while pretending you totally know where you’re going. For students, that can already be a lot. But what about the teachers who are new to CPEC? [BR] [BR]

We decided to get to know some of our newer teachers a little better, not just by asking how their first few weeks have gone, but also by finding out what they want to do in the future and what they eat when they need some comfort. [BR] [BR]

Luckily, it seems like CPEC has been pretty easy to settle into for these new teachers. [BR] [BR]

In the interview from the CPEC Times, Dr. Ozen said “I enjoy being here at CPEC so the transition was smooth, I feel supported by the colleagues and principal turner has made the transition easier for me.” [BR] [BR]

Mr. Mojica had an interesting comparison because he came from another school district in North Carolina. He said “For me, the transition is not that hard because I came from a different district but still located here in North Carolina, so the difference is not that big. But in terms of the behavior of the students, the work ethic is very different compared to where I came from. Students here are more behaved, more respectful, and management is not an issue. So the transition was very easy for me; it didn’t take me more than a week to adjust because, again, I've been saying this, everything here is kind of perfect, like the school community. I like the teachers; they’re very friendly and very supportive, especially Mr. Freeman and Ms. Rutledge since they are in my department. So, everything went smoothly." [BR] [BR]

Then there’s Mr. Arnold, who has had the unique experience of teaching mostly freshmen while also being new to the school himself. He said “The transition to CPEC has been great. The staff and students here are awesome and have been very welcoming. It's been great to teach the majority of freshmen because we’re all learning the school at the same time. I think from a support standpoint everything's been great.” [BR] [BR]

But being a teacher at CPEC might not necessarily be their forever job. [BR] [BR]

Dr. Ozen said “Certainly yes, I’ve been teaching for 20 years in both high school and college classes; I would stay for another 5 years.” [BR]

Mr. Mojica has a slightly different plan. While he enjoys teaching and plans to continue for now, he doesn't see himself staying in the classroom forever. He told the Times “I’m going to be honest: I think I won’t be a teacher my whole life, because I want to explore some other jobs, like becoming a flight attendant or working in the bank. But for now, of course, I love teaching, especially now that I’m in Central Piedmont Early College. Actually, my passion for teaching came back because of the way these students are behaving in class, the way they are engaged, and their willingness to work as well, which really ignites again the passion that I have for teaching. So yeah, for now, of course, I’ll keep teaching as long as I can, but just like what I said at first, I will not retire as a teacher. I would like to explore some other jobs as well.” [BR] [BR]

Mr. Arnold also plans to stay in the classroom for the short and medium term. He said “I see myself staying in the classroom for the short and medium term. If I were ever to leave the classroom, it would probably be for an administrator position, and in order to do that, I would have to go back to school; I’m not exactly sure how attractive that is at this stage in my life.” [BR] [BR]

And then we get to the question that really matters: What is your comfort food? [BR] [BR]

Dr. Ozen didn't even have to think about this one. He was eating it during the interview: sushi. More specifically, cooked sushi, because he doesn't eat raw meat. Apparently, the secret ingredient is soy sauce, which he says makes it “a lot better.” Honestly, he might be onto something. [BR] [BR]

Mr. Mojica's answer was a little more budget-friendly. His comfort food is the five-dollar meal from McDonald's. Nuggets, a burger, fries, and a drink that can be switched to coffee? Sounds like a pretty complete meal. It also reminds him of the Philippines since McDonald's is there too, which makes the meal a little more meaningful than just a quick trip through the drive-thru. [BR] [BR]

Mr. Arnold's answer has probably the biggest story behind it. His go-to comfort food are potatoes, but not just any potatoes. His grandmother grew up in the South and used to cook them in bacon grease with onions and peppers. He remembers how good they were and still makes them himself. He said he can get them almost perfectly like his grandmother did, although her scratch biscuits and her chicken and dumplings are still a work in progress.
Three teachers, three very different career plans, and three very different comfort foods. Whether they're planning on staying at CPEC for years or eventually moving on to something completely different, it's nice to know a little more about the people standing at the front of our classrooms. [BR] [BR]

So if you ever see Dr. Ozen with sushi, Mr. Mojica with a McDonald's bag, or Mr. Arnold making potatoes, now you'll know why.
