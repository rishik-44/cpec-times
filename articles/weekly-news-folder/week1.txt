[HEADING: To Pound or Not to Pound? What is the Outlook for the 2026 Carolina Panthers?]

by Austin Hall [BR] [BR]

Last season, the Carolina Panthers only won eight games, yet were still able to make the playoffs due to 
them beating both the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and the Atlanta Falcons in tie breakers. Can quarterback Bryce 
Young lead the Panthers to another playoff appearance? Or will the Panthers fall short yet again? [BR][BR]

[IMAGE: temporary-portrait.jpg]
[CAPTION: Dense Jungle filled with huge trees]
According to an article from Yahoo! Sports written by Bryce Lazenby, the Panthers are expected to regress. 
There are many reasons for this. First of all, the Panthers have the third-toughest schedule in the NFL (Lazenby 2026). 
The Panthers open with the Bears, and they also have to play the Detroit Lions, Philadelphia Eagles, Green Bay Packers, 
Denver Broncos, Baltimore Ravens, Cincinnati Bengals, and the defending Super Bowl champions, the Seattle Seahawks. [BR][BR]

Also, outside of wide receiver Tetairoa McMillan, there isn’t much offensive support for Young. Young played three 
seasons at Alabama before getting drafted by the Panthers with the top overall pick in 2023. While at Alabama, he 
was used to having an offensive line and receivers he could rely on. But he’s not getting this with the Panthers. 
In his first season with Carolina, he got sacked 62 times, which is more than he did in both seasons he started 
with Alabama combined. [BR][BR]

On top of that, there are serious questions about Bryce Young. The former Heisman1 winner was a rookie back in 
2023, but in 2024  he got benched behind Andy Dalton after just two starts, just to be named the starter in 2025 
and lead the Panthers to a playoff berth. There are serious doubts around Young. Last season, Young threw 23 
touchdowns and 11 interceptions, a career high in both categories. With less reliable targets, those touchdown numbers 
will decrease, and sacks and interceptions will go up. [BR][BR]

Some fans have said that rookie quarterback Haynes King should start over Young, but that would just make the problem 
worse. Haynes King was a mediocre quarterback last year when he played college football with Georgia Tech. Although 
he did lead Georgia Tech to a great season where they finished ranked in both the AP and Coach’s top 25, he didn’t do 
anything great. Georgia Tech had a great running back in Justice Hayes and other great receivers. Also, the jump from 
college to the NFL is hard for quarterbacks. Quarterbacks like Aaron Murray, Mac Jones, and Stetson Bennett were all 
great in college, some even considered amongst the greatest college quarterbacks in history, but they disappeared when 
they got to the NFL. NFL scouts and coaches know how to recognize talent, and there is a reason why King went undrafted 
and is sitting as quarterback three on the depth chart. [BR][BR]

The outlook this season for the Panthers this year definitely isn’t great. Currently, the Buccaneers are the favorite 
to win the NFC South (Lazenby 2026). The Panthers have the third best odds (they are above the Falcons, but below the 
New Orleans Saints), but the Falcons could definitely give the Panthers, Saints, and the Buccaneers a run for their money. 
First of all, they have great depth at quarterback with Tua Tagovailoa (pronounced tag-o-vie-low-uh) and Michael Penix, 
plus a great running back and a great wide receiver room. In the NFL, you play your division opponents twice, so on top 
of the teams mentioned earlier, they also have to play the Bucs, Saints, and Falcons twice. With only a couple of good 
players on offense and a solid defense, the Carolinas shouldn’t expect the Panthers to make the playoffs again this year. [BR]
