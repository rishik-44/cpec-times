[HEADING: Freshman Year]

By Marley Cruz[BR][BR]



Freshman year comes with many new experiences: new classes, new teachers, new people, and trying to figure out where everything is. 
Now that we are almost halfway through the first quarter, it felt like the perfect time to check in with some freshmen to see how 
their first year at CPEC is going! [BR]

For the most part the freshmen seem to be looking forward to this year with some great reviews.[BR][BR]

“I really like freshman year so far!” Cynthia said. She truly enjoys the campus, environment, people, and teachers, saying her 
experience so far has been “really good.” [BR]

Forrest had a slightly more analytical take. According to him the classes have been well balanced, although the work load was noticeably 
heavier at the beginning, eventually “tapering down.”[BR]

Janaye and Saniyah gave simple but positive responses with Janaye saying this year has been “so calm and collective” with Saniyah saying,
 “I like freshman year so far, it’s going great!”[BR]

Joshua on the other hand had plenty to say about his experience. He described freshman year as “top tier: and it has been especially fun
 due to the fact that not many people from his middle school are here. He feel quite welcomed by the school environment.[BR]

Maeve said her year seems to be starting off well. She said she likes CPEC better than her old school, has met a lot of great people, and 
thinks the teachers are nice. [BR]

Then we have “Larp” when asked about freshman year he said “Good.” You know how the saying goes, silence is true wisdom's best reply. [BR][BR]



Its great to see that the freshmen seem to be adjusting well with plenty still figuring things out which is where the CPEC Peer Mentorship 
Program comes in! [BR]

The Peer Mentorship Program connects freshmen with older students who can help them navigate the transition from middle school into 
the double life of high school and college. Mentors are there to be an advocate, a source of advice and even a friend for their mentees. 
The program also holds events throughout the year, giving mentors and mentee opportunities to hang out, bond, and take a break from the 
stress of school. [BR]

For some freshmen, having a mentor to turn to could be especially helpful. [BR][BR]

Cynthia said she is still a little confused about how schedules work beyond freshman year and thinks having a mentor could help her 
understand what to expect. She also hopes the program will help her with managing her work, trouble asking for help, and staying on top 
of her responsibilities. [BR]

Tommy had a similar view, saying the program is “a really good opportunity for freshmen to get to know more people” and get help from an 
older student when they’re struggling with classes or simply trying to navigate their new environment. [BR]

Forrest also sees the program as a good way for freshmen to connect with other students. He pointed out that mentors getting to choose 
their mentees is neat, he jokingly adds, “hopefully no one picks their arch enemies." (Thankfully it seems like no one hasn’t!) Forrest 
appreciates how nice his mentor is and is glad his friends have good mentors as well.[BR][BR]

Having this program seems to already be helping our freshmen while giving them information from someone who has already been through the 
confusing parts of starting at CPEC. Whether a student needs help understanding their schedule, wants advice about classes, or just needs 
someone to talk to, having an older student around can make the transition a little easier. [BR][BR]

Glad to say that our freshmen are doing just fine at the moment. [BR][BR]

Well… most, we still have Larp who has yet to state more on his current thoughts of this school year. Maybe he is exercising his 5th 
amendment? Or just practicing for when he is famous and surrounded by paparazzi?[BR][BR][BR][BR]

[HEADING: 25 Years But Never Forget ] 
by Cole Botzenmayer [BR] [BR]

On September 11, 2026, the United States marked the 25th anniversary of one of the most significant and life-changing events in American history.
 These terrorist attacks by al-Qaeda, commonly known as 9/11, changed the nation forever and still have a lasting impact on American life, security,
  and culture today. [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: https://www.detroitnews.com/gcdn/-mm-/17e8aa54082b0be812c1c06963b9d74ad2af490d/c=0-143-2048-1300/local/-/media/2015/09/10/DetroitNews/DetroitNews/635775255849906714-getty911-1.jpg?width=700&height=396&fit=crop&format=pjpg&auto=webp]
[CAPTION: Moments before the second plane crashed into the South Tower [BR] Photo Credits: Detroit News]

On the morning of September 11, 2001, four American Airlines and United Airlines planes were hijacked. American Airlines Flight 11  flew into the 
North Tower at 8:46 AM, and United Airlines Flight 175  struck the South Tower at 9:03 AM. The third plane, American Airlines Flight 77, was also 
hijacked and crashed into the western side of the Pentagon at 9:37 AM. The last plane, United Airlines Flight 93, was hijacked and was believed to 
be headed for the U.S. Capitol Building or the White House, but brave passengers fought back and prevented the terrorists from reaching their target. 
The plane crashed near Shanksville, Pennsylvania at 10:03 AM. Imagine what it felt like for four terrorist attacks to happen all in one morning, 
especially for those whose loved ones died that treacherous morning. [BR] [BR]

Many of our own parents remember this day from high school or college. For many Americans, 9/11 was a defining moment in history. The attacks led to 
major changes in airport security, intelligence gathering, and national defense. In the months following the attacks, the U.S. passed the Patriot Act, 
which expanded government surveillance and law enforcement powers to detect and prevent future terrorist attacks. The U.S. also enacted the Homeland 
Security Act, which resulted in the creation of the Department of Homeland Security. In the nearly 250 years America has existed, few events have had 
as great an impact on the security of the United States as 9/11. [BR] [BR]

After the attacks of 9/11, Americans were left asking, "What now?" The events of that day changed everyday life across the country. People became more 
concerned about their safety and security, especially when traveling. Airport procedures became much stricter, and security checks became a normal part 
of flying. Many Americans also became more aware of the threat of terrorism and the importance of being prepared for emergencies. The attacks changed how 
people viewed their safety and the world around them. [BR] [BR]

9/11 also had a major impact on American culture. In the weeks and months following the attacks, patriotism increased across the country as American 
flags were displayed on homes, businesses, and public buildings. Communities came together to support victims' families, first responders, and those 
affected by the attacks. The events of 9/11 influenced movies, television shows, music, and education, shaping how future generations learned about 
terrorism and national security. [BR] [BR]

Twenty-five years later, 9/11 remains one of the most important events in American history. The attacks changed the nation's security, everyday life, 
and culture. As Americans remember this anniversary, they continue to honor the victims, the heroes, and the lessons learned from that tragic day. [BR] [BR]

[HEADING: Football Icon Retires From International Duty]
by Carlos Aguilar [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/0fc6b8d98f8d39c88d7f18b48f38b9c18c1c87ce/737_105_2869_2295/master/2869.jpg?width=620&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none]
[CAPTION: Messi after losing the 2026 FIFA World Cup to Spain in East Rutherford, New Jersey. [BR] Photo Credits: Paul Ellis/AFP/Getty Images]

On August 31st, 2026, football legend Lionel Messi announced his retirement from the Argentine national team. After winning just about every trophy the 
sport has to offer, Messi has decided that the time was right to stop playing internationally. This decision has come at a very difficult time for him, 
as his father passed away only weeks prior, but at the age of 39, Lionel Messi has already done everything one could do for their national team. [BR] [BR]

Although being one of the greatest players in the sport’s history, for a long time Messi could not find international success. It got to the point that 
Messi briefly retired from the national team in 2016 following a heartbreaking loss in the Copa America final, but he soon came back and finally won his 
first major international title with Argentina in 2021, winning the Copa America. The following year, Messi led Argentina to a historic World Cup trophy 
in Qatar, winning Argentina’s first World Cup in almost 40 years and third total victory in the tournament. To top it off, Messi’s Argentina won the 
Copa America again in 2024, going back to back in the tournament, and cementing his legacy as a legend for Argentina. Even at the age of 39, Messi put 
on an incredible performance at the 2026 World Cup, briefly becoming the all time top scorer in the tournament’s history and leading his country to yet 
another final. [BR] [BR]

All in all, Lionel Messi is an Argentine legend, and his legacy will be remembered for years to come. He has retired from international football with 
nothing left to prove and as one of the sport's best players ever.

[HEADING: To Pound or Not to Pound? What is the Outlook for the 2026 Carolina Panthers?]
by Austin Hall [BR] [BR]

Last season, the Carolina Panthers only won eight games, yet were still able to make the playoffs due to them beating both the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and 
the Atlanta Falcons in tie breakers. Can quarterback Bryce Young lead the Panthers to another playoff appearance? Or will the Panthers fall short yet again? [BR] [BR]

According to an article from Yahoo! Sports written by Bryce Lazenby, the Panthers are expected to regress. There are many reasons for this. First of all, 
the Panthers have the third-toughest schedule in the NFL (Lazenby 2026). The Panthers open with the Bears, and they also have to play the Detroit Lions, 
Philadelphia Eagles, Green Bay Packers, Denver Broncos, Baltimore Ravens, Cincinnati Bengals, and the defending Super Bowl champions, the Seattle Seahawks. [BR] [BR]

[IMAGE: https://www.charlotteobserver.com/public/latest-news/mxvj25/picture312204208/alternates/LANDSCAPE_768/Tet1.jpg]
[CAPTION: McMillan (left) and Young (right) greeting each other prior to their week 3 game against the Falcons. [BR] Photo Credits: Cory Knowlton via the Charlotte Observer]

Photo Credits: Cory Knowlton via the Charlotte Observer [BR]
Also, outside of wide receiver Tetairoa McMillan, there isn’t much offensive support for Young. Young played three seasons at Alabama before getting drafted by the Panthers 
with the top overall pick in 2023. While at Alabama, he was used to having an offensive line and receivers he could rely on. But he’s not getting this with the Panthers. In 
his first season with Carolina, he got sacked 62 times, which is more than he did in both seasons he started with Alabama combined. [BR] [BR]

On top of that, there are serious questions about Bryce Young. The former Heisman1 winner was a rookie back in 2023, but in 2024  he got benched behind Andy Dalton after just 
two starts, just to be named the starter in 2025 and lead the Panthers to a playoff berth. There are serious doubts around Young. In his article on Yahoo! Sports, Lazenby wrote
 “[Young] went from being benched in 2024 to having his career-best season in 2025. Not everybody believes in Young, though, and if he fails to repeat or improve on his 2025 
 performance, it's not hard to imagine the Panthers struggling.” Last season, Young threw 23 touchdowns and 11 interceptions, a career high in both categories. With less reliable 
 targets, those touchdown numbers will decrease, and sacks and interceptions will go up. [BR] [BR]

The outlook this season for the Panthers this year definitely isn’t great. Currently, the Buccaneers are the favorite to win the NFC South (Lazenby 2026). The Panthers 
have the third best odds (they are above the Falcons, but below the New Orleans Saints), but the Falcons could definitely give the Panthers, Saints, and the Buccaneers a 
run for their money. First of all, they have great depth at quarterback with Tua Tagovailoa (pronounced tag-o-vie-low-uh) and Michael Penix, plus a great running back and 
a great wide receiver room. In the NFL, you play your division opponents twice, so on top of the teams mentioned earlier, they also have to play the Bucs, Saints, and 
Falcons twice. With only a couple of good players on offense and a solid defense, the Carolinas shouldn’t expect the Panthers to make the playoffs again this year. [BR] [BR]

1The Heisman is an award given to the best college football player, usually a quarterback, at the end of each season. [BR] [BR] [BR]
